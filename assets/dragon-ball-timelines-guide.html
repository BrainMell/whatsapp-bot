MODULE 01

📜 The Age System & Master Chronology

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=01

🗓️What is an "Age"?

Dragon Ball runs on an in-universe calendar counting years as Ages — roughly our Gregorian years. The system was formalized by the Daizenshuu databooks (the seven-volume encyclopedia Shueisha published in 1995–96, with Akira Toriyama's direct involvement) and has been the backbone every later guidebook, video game and wiki has built on. Nearly every timeline explainer video you'll find on YouTube ultimately recites — or argues with — the Daizenshuu 7 chronology, translated for Western fans by Kanzenshuu.

Two things surprise most first-time viewers. First, the "future" of the franchise is extremely close: the original manga's epilogue (the 28th World Martial Arts Tournament, where Goku meets Uub) is dated Age 784 — just ten years after the Buu battle. Second, the deep past got a massive expansion by Dragon Ball Daima (2024–25), which formalized the "Divine Calendar" of the Demon Realm and pinned down creation lore: the multiverse was created by Rymus at the Demon King's command, the Super Dragon Balls were formed by Zalama in Divine Year 41, and the Namekians are refugees from the Demon Realm.

Cross-check note: the official Dragon Ball portal site publishes news, merchandise and event pages but hosts no canon chronology — the Ages people argue about online come from the databooks (Daizenshuu / Chōzenshū), film production materials, and games. That's why every placement debate in Module 07 traces back to which of those sources you trust.

🧬 Canon Layers — What "Counts"

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=02

🥸The four-layer model most explainers converge on

Ask ten YouTubers "what is Dragon Ball canon?" and you'll get eleven answers — but the structure of their answers is almost always the same four-layer onion, because it's the only way to reconcile Toriyama's manga with the enormous media empire built around it. The top 50 timeline videos overwhelmingly organize their content this way: first sort the media, then place it in the Ages.

✅ Universally acceptedThe manga; Super's Beerus/Broly/Super Hero films (Toriyama wrote them); Minus; Jaco.
🟡 Accepted with asterisksSuper anime (watched as continuity by most, contested by manga-first fans due to divergences); History of Trunks; Daima (Toriyama's final credited story work).
🟠 Timeline-critical but animeThe Z-era pieces that define the future timelines: History of Trunks (TV special) and the manga's own Trunks epilogue — the manga is Layer 1, the special is Layer 3, and they disagree on dates (Module 05).
❌ Not canon, but functionalGT (anime-original, described by Toriyama in later interviews as an anime "side story" — paraphrase; he drew designs for it but didn't write it). Games like Xenoverse and Heroes are self-aware time-tourism.

One more modern complication worth its own paragraph: the franchise now has two Super continuities running in parallel — the manga (Toyotarou, continuing past where the 2018 anime stopped, through Moro, Granolah, Super Hero and a new arc) and, as of October 2026, a brand-new "Dragon Ball Super: Beerus" anime that Kanzenshuu reports debuted this month and retells the Battle of Gods era. Timeline videos made before 2026 obviously don't cover it, which is exactly why the "newest placement" explainers (Carthu's Dojo's "NEW Dragon Ball Timeline") keep getting re-uploaded — the target keeps moving.

MODULE 03

⏳ Two Time-Travel Engines

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=03

🛠️Engine 1: Trunks' time machine (Z, Cell arc)

Future Trunks' Capsule Corp machine runs on the rule Bulma states mid-Cell-arc, and which every serious explainer treats as Toriyama's own word on the subject: you cannot change your own past. Traveling back doesn't rewrite history — it spawns a new parallel world that splits at your arrival, while your home world continues exactly as before, forever doomed. This is why Future Trunks' warning to Goku (Age 764) saves the manga's world but not his own — the manga drives this home brutally in its epilogue, where Trunks returns home anyway and has to save his own world by himself.

Consequences that follow directly: every round trip the machine makes creates or re-enters branches; your home timeline can only be fixed from inside it; and there is no single "true" history — only a growing tree. Fuel is the practical limiter (Bulma hunts for a power source in both Z and Super; it's why Trunks can't just hop around freely), though fans note the limiter is soft — Trunks manages three trips in Z and another in Super.

💍Engine 2: the Kaioshin Time Rings (Super, Future Trunks saga)

When Super needed time travel again for the Goku Black arc, it didn't reuse the machine's rules — it revealed that Supreme Kais carry Time Rings, and Whis/Shin explain their physics: a Ring takes you to the past of a parallel world, never your own history; changes made there never propagate back; and a Ring physically reacts (glows green) when it detects that "history has been altered" somewhere. Super's arc treats this as the reason Beerus destroying the main timeline's Zamasu did not save Future Trunks' world — that Zamasu came from yet another parallel world entirely.

The Dragon Ball Wiki's Alternate Timeline article frames Trunks' Z-era machine trips in exactly these terms — each trip "altered" a timeline and spawned a new branch, retroactively decorating the Z-era story with Super-era Ring mechanics (their Age 764 article notes a "Green Time Ring" was effectively created by Trunks' alteration).

⚠️ DEBATE — do the two engines actually contradict each other?

Majority view: No contradiction — both engines implement the same "branching parallel worlds" rule; Rings are just the official, god-sanctioned version, and the machine is the rogue version. Super is restating Toriyama's Z-era model with better special effects.
Alternative reading: The mechanics don't match in the details — the machine physically departs/arrives (it's found in ruins with Cell!), creates a branch at arrival, and crosses decades; Rings create no new branch (they visit "the past of a parallel world" that already exists) and are subject to Zeno-era enforcement (history "corrections"). Under a strict reading they behave like different devices, and the arc's own ending (Module 06) muddies it further.
Evidence anchors: Bulma's Z-era explanation vs Whis/Shin's Ring briefing; the wiki's framing of the machine trips as branch-creators; and the long-running fan complaint threads this guide's video list keeps surfacing (e.g. Laughingstock Media's "HOW Does TIME TRAVEL Work In Dragon Ball Z?"). Both camps agree on the headline rule: the past you visit is never your own.
MODULE 04

🐉 The Cell Arc: Four Timelines

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=04 --diagram=branchmap

🌍The branching — as the sources stabilize it

Here is the heart of every Dragon Ball timeline discussion. The Cell arc quietly builds a four-world multiverse, and the authoritative reading — the one the Daizenshuu 7 diagram encodes and the Dragon Ball Wiki's Alternate Timeline article maintains — counts four distinct timelines. One of them is never shown on screen at all. Labeling is a mess across sources (the wiki numbers them 1–4; forum posts use letters; videos invent their own names), so this guide uses fixed letters:

TIMELINE A — The Manga TimelineThe one we watch. Created by Future Trunks' first machine trip; later also receives Cell.
TIMELINE B — Future Trunks' HomeHistory of Trunks + the manga's epilogue. The "original" history Goku's world branched away from.
TIMELINE C — Cell's Home ("the unseen timeline")Nearly identical to B — but there, Cell won. His Trunks is dead; his time machine is stolen.
TIMELINE D — The Never-Seen PastThe world Timeline C's Trunks visited to get strong enough to beat his androids. Exists purely by inference.

🔍Walking each lane

Timeline A (manga): Trunks-B arrives in Age 764 — this arrival is the branch point that "creates" A — kills Mecha-Frieza, and warns Goku. Cell-C lands the same year and buries himself to power down for three years (his line when Piccolo finds him in Age 767 checks out against a 764 landing). The androids wake May 12, 767; Cell absorbs them; the Cell Games end with Gohan's Father-Son Kamehameha on May 26, 767. Trunks-B then goes home.

Timeline B: Goku dies of the heart virus around Age 767; the androids spend 18 years grinding the world down. Gohan dies in Age 780 (Trunks is 14 — his birth year 766 makes the special's math exact). Bulma finishes the machine; Trunks departs at 18 (Age 784), makes two visits, and returns home in Age 785 strong enough to destroy #17 and #18. Three years later, in Age 788, the manga's epilogue shows him destroying his own world's Cell — which never time-traveled because Trunks was there to stop it.

Timeline C: Identical to B on the surface — androids destroyed in Age 785 — but in Age 788 the Cell fight goes the other way. Cell kills Trunks, steals the machine, and flies to Age 764 of Timeline A. His stated motive: with the androids gone, there was nothing left for him to absorb in his own world.

Timeline D: The wiki's "never seen" world — the past that Trunks-C visited on his own round trip, which is how he came home strong enough to beat his androids. Everything about D beyond that one sentence is projection, which is exactly why it's the community's favorite toy box.

🧨Why do B and C differ if they're "identical"? The remote-control theory

Since B and C look the same until Age 788, the fight outcome needs a cause. The manga never explains it outright, so the fandom built the most-cited bridge: in the Cell arc, Bulma builds an emergency stop controller designed to shut the androids down. The theory says Timeline B's Trunks won by raw power trained across two past trips (including surviving the Cell Games era), while Timeline C's Trunks — with only one trip, to D — may have won his android fight by other means (a controller-like device, or simply less Cell-Game-forged experience), and therefore lost his Age 788 duel. Explainer videos keep this theory alive; the wiki keeps it out of its main articles as speculation. It's the single best example of how Dragon Ball timeline lore is really written: canon gives the state machine, the fandom fills in the transition table.

⚠️ DEBATE PACK — the Cell arc's four live arguments

Four timelines (majority): A, B, C, and the inferred D — matching the Daizenshuu diagram and the wiki. Cell's landing doesn't split A, because arrivals don't branch destinations.
Three timelines (the minimalist camp): Cell's arrival in Age 764 of A is itself an unbookkept alteration — if Trunks' arrival created A, Cell's arrival can't coexist in the same branch unless it happened after Trunks' (same year, unverified order!). Minimalists either accept A got created twice (a contradiction) or quietly drop D.
Ordering in Age 764 (mainstream): Trunks arrives first, Cell lands shortly after — the Daizenshuu diagram's spacing, which keeps A a single coherent world (Cell just lives in it underground). Videos like Qaaman's "UNSEEN TIMELINE" deep-dives defend this bookkeeping.
Ordering skeptic: Nothing on-panel pins the order within the year. If Cell landed first, the manga world is actually a fifth timeline (Cell's landing creating an A′ that Trunks' Trip 1 then forks) — and the tree is taller than advertised. SpellCommander91's "All 25 Timelines" video is the maximalist endpoint of this logic.
"Why not warn earlier?" (mainstream answer): Fuel is scarce (Bulma's hunt in both Z and Super), and any earlier arrival just creates another doomed branch — you cannot save your own world from the past, so a bigger margin wouldn't help B at all.
The pushback: Trunks nonetheless makes three Z-era trips plus a Super trip, so the fuel limit is soft; and "saving another world" is morally identical to saving yours — the characters keep doing it. The in-universe answer is emotional (Trunks' own words about protecting his home), not mechanical.
Evidence anchors: Alternate Timeline (DB Wiki) · r/dragonball "How was Timeline 4 dealt with?" threads · Daizenshuu 7 diagram (Kanzenshuu-translated) · the manga epilogue's Age 785/788 dates.

The Dragon Ball Wiki's timeline pages quietly treat the manga as authoritative wherever the two disagree — which is also where the community landed after years of arguing.

⚠️ DEBATE — "the original timeline" mindset

Majority view: Timeline B is the original history — the unbranched trunk where Goku dies, the androids win, and Trunks' interference only ever produces better worlds elsewhere. The manga's world is the fork; ours, not theirs, is the "changed" copy. Videos like Laughingstock Media's "What Happened In The ORIGINAL TIMELINE" build entire retrospectives on this poignancy.
Alternative reading: "Original" is a misnomer — under branching physics there is no trunk, only a family tree with no root label. The emotional reading is fine; the mechanical one is not. BlazingAuras' "The Original Timeline of DRAGON BALL was INSANE" and similar videos lean into the trunk framing because it makes better storytelling.
Evidence anchors: Bulma's branching speech (manga); the wiki's alternate-timeline structure; the epilogue dates above. Both camps agree Trunks' world cannot be "saved" from the past — only from inside (or by Zeno, catastrophically — Module 06).
MODULE 06

🌹 Goku Black & the Ring Multiverse

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=06 --diagram=zamasu-map

🌀Three Zamasus, two murders of history, one erasure

Super's Future Trunks saga is the franchise's second great timeline event, and it works by introducing a new rule (Module 03's Time Rings) and then weaponizing it: because the past you visit is a parallel world's past, your own world's evils stay undefeated. The arc runs on three Zamasus — the main timeline's (dealt with quickly), an unseen world's (who becomes Goku Black), and Future Trunks' own (who becomes immortal). The Dragon Ball Wiki's Alternate Timeline article treats the resulting mess as the arc's structural spine, and explainer videos treat it as their favorite paradox (Geekdom101's "Zamasu's Time Loop" and Kameha Man's "Zamasu's Time Paradox" are built on it).

🧮The bookkeeping this arc creates

Counting strictly: the Cell arc gave us A/B/C/D; the Black arc adds the unseen origin world ②, Trunks' Super-era world (a continuation of B), the post-erasure nothing, and the new copy-world branch B′ — while the main timeline keeps rolling. Maximalist counters (the "25 timelines" school of video essay) also tally every Ring trip and every implication; minimalists argue only ② and B′ are genuinely new worlds. Nobody's count is "wrong"; the canon simply refuses to publish a ledger.

⚠️ DEBATE PACK — the Black arc's live arguments

Copy-world ending (majority): It's a bittersweet but legal solution — Whis explicitly picks "a world almost identical," so Trunks & Mai get a home while the rules stay intact. It honors the one law the franchise keeps repeating: the past you save is never your own.
The objection: It quietly manufactures an infinite multiverse (any world can be re-run as a copy), it dooms that copy world's Trunks to lose his home by substitution (or does he grow up alongside our Trunks? the anime doesn't say), and it makes Beerus' earlier "clean" fix of Zamasu ① look arbitrary. Videos like Carthu's "Saving Dragon Ball's Future Timeline" pitch alternatives — restoration, repopulation, Time-Ring loops — because the canon ending leaves the erased world permanently dark.
Why Beerus' fix didn't help (mainstream): Rings/branches, full stop — the arc explains it on screen, and it's the cleanest rule demonstration in the franchise.
The plot-hole hunt: If Zamasu ② came from an unseen world, which Trip or Ring moved him to Trunks' world — and why does Trunks' world have a Time Machine-shaped hole in its defenses (Bulma's present-day machine needed for the rescue) when Rings were always available to gods? The wiki documents the mechanics; the "why" is fandom territory.
Evidence anchors: Alternate Timeline (DB Wiki) · the anime's Whis/Shin Ring briefing · Geekdom101's and Kameha Man's paradox explainers (Module 10, videos #18 & #26).
MODULE 07

📅 Super's Placement & the End-of-Z Crunch

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=07

🧗Ten years, one wall

Everything Super does must fit between the Buu battle (Age 774) and the 28th Budokai (Age 784) — the "End of Z" epilogue, where Goku leaves with Uub and the designs age up. That wall is why placement is the most fought-over real estate in the fandom, and why the wiki's own timeline page opens with a list of things it refuses to reconcile.

🟣 Battle of Gods — Age 778Guidebook date. Weekly Jump's "6 months after Buu" (→ Age 774–775) contradicts it; the wiki follows the databooks.
🟣 Resurrection 'F — Age 779Frieza's return; Future Trunks saga follows in 779–780 (Module 06).
🟣 Broly — Age 780Film materials anchor it; the same film's flashbacks (Age 732/737) reshuffled Bardock lore (Module 08).
❓ Super Hero — Age 782 or 783?The wiki hosts an "Age 783" page; community threads argue for 782 with equal heat. No on-screen year card settles it.

⚠️ DEBATE PACK — can Super still "reach" End of Z?

The optimists: There's room — Super Hero's era leaves a year before Age 784, the manga can compress arcs, and Super's own final scenes (Goku farming, Vegeta in a polo) already walk the designs toward End of Z. Some fans even read the Super manga's later arcs as deliberately clearing the runway.
The skeptics: Power creep has erased End of Z's stakes (why does Goku care about the 28th Budokai if he's fought angels?), the crunchy continuity details multiply (Goten/Trunks' ages and designs, Gohan's scholar life, the never-explained quiet decade), and every new arc makes the wall heavier. This camp expects the story to simply stop short of 784 forever.
Evidence anchors: the wiki timeline page's exclusion notes (quoted above); the Super Hero year threads; and the endlessly re-uploaded "updated timeline" videos (Module 10, videos #8 & #39) whose very existence proves the target keeps moving. Add to it the new "Super: Beerus" anime (October 2026) retelling the BoG era — placement discourse is being re-litigated in real time.

🔄 The Retcon Map

rendered from .j proto dbtimelines --module=08

👶Bardock: one year (Age 737), two incompatible stories

No year in the franchise has been rewritten harder. The 1990 TV special made Bardock a visionary who defies Frieza alone and dies cursing him, sending baby Kakarot away in a final act of rebellion. Then Dragon Ball Minus (2014, the bonus chapter of the Jaco volume) and Broly (2018) rebuilt the departure as a quiet parental decision: Bardock and Gine, worried because Frieza has started treating Saiyans strangely, put Kakarot in a pod themselves — no psychic visions, no last stand, and (per the Broly film) Bardock's fate is simply "killed with the planet." The Dragon Ball Wiki's master timeline literally files all three stories under Age 737 side by side, and adds a birth-year footnote: in Minus/Broly, Goku was born Age 734 and spent three years in his nursing capsule before launch, where the classic databooks say Age 737 outright. The community's peace treaty: Minus/Broly wins (it's Toriyama-authored and Layer 2), the special survives as an emotionally beloved Layer 3 "what if" — and video essays like the "entire Bardock timeline" genre exist to reconcile them anyway.

That's why "timeline explained" content never goes stale: each retcon refreshes the whole genre. The Bardock special and History of Trunks are the only Layer 3 pieces so load-bearing that the canon visibly grew around them rather than over them.
